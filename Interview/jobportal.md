
LINKEDIN
==================


LinkedIn is potentially the largest careers database, with more than 700 million users on the platform.  It’s not just individuals however: companies also use LinkedIn to showcase their corporate brand.  

You can use the platform to research companies and gain a better understanding of both operations and culture, through their company pages.  You can also research the people who work there, including their job functions or roles, qualifications, and what skills they have.  This can help you to understand the needs of the organisation and the role and enable you to demonstrate your match with the role more effectively.

Following organisations and individuals can help you keep up to date with new developments and trends, not just in a specific organisation, but in the industry or sector that you are targeting.  Joining LinkedIn groups allows you to start conversations with people who have similar professional interests and can help with your research.  Having a good understanding of the environment that an organisation operates in can enable you demonstrate your fit more effectively at interview and show you as a proactive and well-informed candidate.

Your LinkedIn account means that you can research interviewers and hiring managers before attending interviews.  You may find that you have connections in common, have worked in the same organisations or share similar interests or backgrounds.

You can also use LinkedIn to target people within your network who currently work for organisations that you would like to work for, or those who have worked for them in the past.  These people can be useful networking contacts to answer your questions and provide you with information that may help you at interview, and when you are considering an offer.


INDEED.COM
==================

You may already be using Indeed to search for jobs, but did you know it also provides information to help you answer salary questions?  When you get to the main page, look at the bottom left for the “salaries” link. You can then enter a position title or a company name and Indeed will take you to a page with salary averages across the country and in specific locations. Use the average salary for your position and location to determine a suitable salary range that reflects your level of experience and success.

You can also use Indeed to browse companies and gather information on hiring trends.


GLASSDOOR.COM
==================

Glassdoor gathers information from past and present employees of various companies to provide valuable insights that can help you decide if a company is a good fit for you. You can use Glassdoor to:

Check out a company’s culture. From the menu (top left) on the main page, click on “Company Reviews” and insert a company name and location in the search boxes. Glassdoor will take you to a page where you can select what you want to see about that company. Click on Reviews and read up on how current and past employees rate the company, the CEO, and their experience working there.
Become familiar with the company’s interviewing practices. You will get information on the interview experience (positive, neutral or negative), how previous interviewees have landed an interview with the company, and even sample interview questions!
Gather/confirm salary information. Glassdoor provides salary information for the company across the country and in specific locations, by position title. As you did with Indeed, remember to consider your experience and success levels when determining the salary range you want to present to employers.
You can see that research is critical to your search and to your interview preparations. Don’t forget to use your network for further insights. Happy researching! 

Reports

From the “Search for a Company” box, just type the name of the company where you will be interviewing and you’ll see several reports you can use to help you prepare. (Bear in mind, large public companies will have more information available than smaller private companies.) Suggested reports include:

SWOT report. Want to get an idea of the company’s Strengths, Weaknesses, Opportunities and Threats? The Company Research Tool provides a brief discussion of each, giving you a better understanding of what you may be headed into, and allowing you to see where you might align/add value in the organisation.  Perhaps you can discuss wins you’ve had in your last job in one of these areas!
News. Clicking on this report will give you recent news stories. Use the filters to reduce the articles to a manageable number. Putting in a search term (like “product launch”) can help you find relevant news articles to read so you can be up to date when you enter the interview.
Review the Competitors Report (listed under “Advanced”) so you are aware of and can discuss the players in this field. This report may help you add companies to your list of potential targets as well. 
Significant Developments. Scroll through this list of headlines to find information relevant to the role you are pursuing. This information is in bite-size pieces that you can search for more detail.
Searching for a technical role? Check out the Technologies in Use report. Being able to discuss the technologies your target company uses will help you connect quickly with your potential peers and leaders.
Industry Snapshot. This short, comprehensive report defines the industry and the players. It also describes what is currently affecting the market and gives Talking Points and Questions Being Asked in the industry. This report is particularly useful if you are interviewing with a company in an industry that is new to you, as it provides industry buzzwords and sources for more information.
