# IFRS, Local GAAP, and Regulatory Reporting Frameworks – Interview Explanation

Financial institutions and enterprises use accounting and reporting frameworks to ensure standardized financial reporting, compliance, taxation, auditability, and regulatory transparency across global and local jurisdictions.

---

# 1. IFRS (International Financial Reporting Standards)

## What is IFRS?

IFRS Foundation IFRS is a global accounting standard used for preparing financial statements consistently across countries.

It focuses on:

* Transparency
* Comparability
* Global financial reporting
* Investor confidence

---

## Core Components of IFRS

| Component                      | Purpose                       |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------- |
| Financial Statements           | Balance sheet, P&L, cash flow |
| Revenue Recognition (IFRS 15)  | Revenue accounting            |
| Lease Accounting (IFRS 16)     | Lease liabilities/assets      |
| Financial Instruments (IFRS 9) | Credit loss & risk            |
| Consolidation                  | Multi-entity reporting        |
| Disclosure Requirements        | Compliance reporting          |

---

# 2. Local GAAP (Generally Accepted Accounting Principles)

## What is Local GAAP?

Local GAAP refers to country-specific accounting rules followed for statutory and legal reporting.

Examples:

* Indian GAAP
* US GAAP
* UK GAAP
* Japanese GAAP

---

## Core Components

| Component                | Purpose                    |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------- |
| Statutory Accounting     | Local legal compliance     |
| Tax Accounting           | Country tax laws           |
| Revenue Recognition      | Country-specific treatment |
| Local Currency Reporting | Domestic reporting         |
| Regulatory Filing        | Government submissions     |

# 3. Regulatory Reporting Frameworks

## What is Regulatory Reporting?

Regulatory reporting ensures organizations submit financial, tax, risk, and compliance reports to regulators.

Financial institutions report to:

* Central banks
* Tax authorities
* Financial regulators
* Stock exchanges

---

## Major Reporting Areas

| Reporting Type   | Purpose                      |
| ---------------- | ---------------------------- |
| Basel Reporting  | Banking risk                 |
| Tax Reporting    | Tax compliance               |
| CbCR             | Country-by-country reporting |
| Pillar Two       | Global minimum tax           |
| ESG Reporting    | Sustainability compliance    |
| Capital Adequacy | Banking solvency             |

---
