Accounts Receivable (AR) process
=================================


The accounts receivable (AR) process involves managing and tracking payments owed by customers for goods or services provided. Here are the key steps in the Accounts Receivable process:

1. Customer Credit Management:

Assess and establish credit limits for customers based on their creditworthiness and payment history.
Determine acceptable payment terms for each customer or client.

2. Invoice Generation:

Create and issue invoices to customers for goods delivered or services rendered.
Ensure accuracy in invoicing, including item details, quantities, prices, taxes, and payment terms.

3. Invoice Delivery:

Send invoices to customers via email, mail, or through online portals.
Confirm receipt of invoices and verify delivery with the customer.

4. Payment Terms Compliance:

Monitor and track adherence to payment terms specified in the invoices.
Send reminders or follow-ups for payments nearing or past due dates.

5. Payment Processing:

Receive payments from customers through various methods (credit card, check, electronic funds transfer, etc.).
Record payments in the accounts receivable system and apply them to the respective invoices.

6. Accounts Reconciliation:

Reconcile received payments with outstanding invoices to ensure accuracy in account balances.
Investigate and resolve discrepancies or unidentified payments.

7. Aging Analysis:

Regularly review aging reports to track the status of outstanding invoices.
Categorize invoices based on the number of days outstanding to prioritize follow-up actions.

8. Collections Management:

Initiate collection efforts for overdue invoices, including sending collection notices, making collection calls, or escalating communication to collect outstanding payments.

9. Dispute Resolution:

Address and resolve any billing disputes or discrepancies raised by customers promptly.
Coordinate with internal departments to clarify or rectify billing issues.

10. Accounting and Reporting:

Update and maintain accurate records of accounts receivable transactions in the accounting system.
Generate regular reports on accounts receivable aging, collections, and cash flow projections.

11. Bad Debt Management:

Identify and assess accounts that may become uncollectible (bad debts) and make provisions accordingly.
Write off uncollectible accounts after exhausting collection efforts.

12. Periodic Review and Improvement:

Conduct periodic reviews of the AR process to identify inefficiencies, bottlenecks, or areas for improvement.
Implement changes or enhancements to streamline processes and enhance collections efficiency.


Efficient management of accounts receivable is crucial for maintaining healthy cash flow and ensuring timely receipt of payments owed by customers or clients. Adjustments to these steps may occur based on specific industry requirements, company policies, or customer relationships.
